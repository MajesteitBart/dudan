# Behavior and limitations

[Back to dudan](../README.md) | [Documentation](README.md)

## Interface and behavior

- Frosted glass cards on a dusk sky, after Superhuman's panels, with components after [beautifului.dev](https://www.beautifului.dev/). Messages sit straight on the sky, which darkens while a chat is open so the text keeps its contrast; the prompt bar is the only glass card on the chat screen. An empty chat shows a large greeting and the prompt bar. The prompt bar holds the message, the attach menu, the model picker, the mic and the send button. Work steps are tool chips, code blocks have a header and line numbers, and approvals have one filled primary choice. Dialogs, sheets and the side-key overlay blur the screen behind them. The Fold 7 inner screen shows the sidebar as a second card; the cover screen gets a full-screen frosted sheet.
- Settings > Appearance > Background picks the sky: Dusk (the default indigo and lavender), Ocean, Forest, Sunset, Graphite or Midnight. The glass, menus, sheets and the overlay take their tint from it.
- Settings > Appearance > Accent color picks one of eight colors (moon white, lavender, blue, teal, green, amber, coral, pink) for the send button, your chat bubbles, links, switches and the glow in the sky.
- Settings > Appearance > Reduce transparency swaps the glass for solid panels. It also switches on by itself when the phone's color contrast (Accessibility > Color and motion) is set to medium or high.
- Agent turns go through Hermes' Runs API. If the phone loses the connection mid-task, the run keeps going on the server and the app polls until the result is in.
- Each turn tells Hermes where it was asked. Hermes' prompt for API server clients says the display is unknown and asks for plain text, so dudan adds a few sentences to every turn: the user is on their phone in dudan, Markdown renders, and, when the reply will be read aloud, write it to be heard. From the side-key overlay they also ask for a short answer and say when the attached image is a screenshot of the screen. A last sentence covers [Phone control](setup.md#phone-control). Hermes adds them to the system prompt for that turn only; they don't end up in the chat history.
- Background work: when the agent hands a task to a subagent in the background, its turn ends right away and Hermes saves the subagent's result in the chat later, without starting a turn. dudan shows that result as a card from the background work, not as your message, and marks failed and partly failed batches. It doesn't continue on its own, because the agent may still need your confirmation, or you may have stopped or changed the task since. Review result and finish asks the agent to act on the result in a normal turn, with the usual approvals, after checking that no other client continued the chat first. While the app runs, it checks the chat for results every few seconds at first and every 30 seconds after that, for up to an hour, and posts one notification per result when dudan isn't on screen. Reopening a chat or returning to the app also shows what arrived meanwhile.
- A collapsible "Worked for 1m 10s" panel shows tool calls, commentary and reasoning. Dangerous commands surface as an approval card with Hermes' choices (once, this chat, always, deny).
- Voice input with a live waveform, read-aloud per reply, and a hands-free Live mode. Speech is transcribed on the phone by [Orukeet](https://github.com/Oruk-AI/orukeet), Oruk's multilingual fine-tune of NVIDIA Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3. It works out which language you speak, which can go wrong on a short sentence; with Settings > Speech input > Speech language set to nl-NL or en-US it sticks to Dutch or English.
- Replies are read aloud in the language they're written in. dudan guesses it per reply: from the script for Korean, Japanese, Arabic, Hindi, Greek and the Cyrillic languages, and from common words and letters only one language uses for the rest. A tie goes to the speech language, then to the phone's own languages. Settings > Voices has a row for each of the phone's languages, one for the speech language, one for English, and one for Other languages. On Automatic, Kokoro-82M reads English and Supertonic 3 reads its 31 languages, Dutch, German, French and Spanish among them, both on the phone; anything else gets the Android voice. Tap a language to pick its engine and voice instead. Only the models the rows need download by themselves: an English phone gets Kokoro, and a Dutch or German one, or one with the speech language set to Dutch or German, gets Supertonic as well. Before Supertonic reads a Dutch reply, the app writes out numbers, times, amounts and dates, because it misreads digits.
- Wake word: with Settings > Assistant > Wake word on, saying "Ok Clark" (or your own phrase) opens the overlay, listening, while the phone is unlocked. A 3.3M-parameter keyword spotter from k2-fsa listens on the phone; nothing is recorded or sent until it hears the phrase. With Orukeet, what you say right after the phrase goes into the question, also without a pause ("Ok Clark what's the weather"), and the phrase itself is left out of it. The Android recognizer plays a short tone once it listens: ask after the tone (see the limitations below). Saying the phrase while the overlay is open listens again in the same chat.
- Clark in the app list: with Settings > Assistant > Clark in the app list on, a second entry named Clark opens the overlay over the current app, listening, also with Listen right away off. Without the assistant role it opens the app, listening, and Settings says so. Switching it on asks for the microphone; if the microphone isn't allowed when Clark opens, the app opens to ask for it. Bixby opens apps by name, so on Samsung "Hi Bixby, open Clark" starts a question without touching the phone. Bixby listens for its own wake word, so with dudan's Wake word off, dudan doesn't keep the microphone open and Android shows no microphone dot for dudan until you ask. The entry is named after the default wake phrase and keeps that name when you change the phrase or the assistant's name, because Android reads an app's name from its manifest.
- Assistant overlay on the side key: starts a new chat on every press, starts listening right away, can attach the current screen ("Ask about screen"), reads spoken questions' answers aloud, and hands off to the full app. Reopening dudan from Recents after you left it with Back starts a new chat too, the way the app icon does, instead of returning to the chat the overlay handed off.
- Model picker backed by Hermes' model inventory. Each default also sets the thinking level (Hermes' own setting, or off up to max) and, for models that support it, fast mode, which asks the provider for priority processing at a higher price. There are two defaults: one for chats started in the app, and a quick one for chats started from the assistant overlay or the assist gesture. A chat keeps the model of the place it started, also when you continue it in the app.
- Files: the attach menu takes photos, videos and any other file up to 250 MB (PDF, Word, spreadsheets, audio, archives), up to eight per message, and other apps can share files to dudan. Photos go to the model as pictures. Other files upload to a small service next to Hermes while you type, and the turn tells the agent where the file is, in the same words Hermes' Telegram and Discord adapters use. The agent then reads, transcribes or inspects it with its own tools. See [Files](setup.md#files).
- Files from the agent: with a compatible Clark server, the agent can return images and other files with its answer. Images show in the chat at their own shape, and Save file puts any of them, APKs too, where you pick with Android's document picker. dudan checks each file's size and SHA-256 before it shows or saves it, and never opens or installs one. See [Files returned by Clark](outbound-attachments.md).
- Rich replies: when a table, chart, steps, choices or a short form help, the agent adds an [OpenUI](https://www.openui.com) block to its answer, and dudan draws it as native UI in the chat. Buttons, follow-up suggestions and form submits send your next message. See [Rich replies](#rich-replies).
- Skills: type `$` in a message and the prompt bar lists Hermes' skills with their descriptions, narrowing as you type. A picked skill goes in as a tag such as `$github-pr-workflow`, in the accent color, and stays colored in the sent message. Tags that match no skill, such as `$5`, stay plain text. The attach menu's Skill entry starts a tag, and the side-key overlay has the same list. The message goes to Hermes as typed. The tagged skills go along in that turn's instructions, after the rich reply rules and the turn's context, and ask the agent to load each skill with `skill_view` before it answers.
- The Skills screen lists Hermes' skills by category. Tap one for its description and Use in chat, which starts a chat with its tag. A Hermes with the [skills patch](../tools/hermes-patches/README.md) also sends the skill's SKILL.md, its path and its extra files. See [Skills](setup.md#skills).
- Search, pin, rename and delete chats. Run, pause or resume scheduled tasks, and add, change or delete them where the server allows it. With Clark, a task's results come to a chat of their own, with a notification. See [Scheduled tasks](#scheduled-tasks).
- Phone control: Hermes can open apps and links, set timers and alarms, and control media on the phone, whichever channel you ask it from. See [Phone control](setup.md#phone-control).
- English and Dutch UI.

## Rich replies

[OpenUI](https://www.openui.com) is an open standard for generative UI: the model writes a compact, line-based language (OpenUI Lang) that names components from a fixed library, and the client renders them. dudan sends Hermes the language rules and the components it draws as extra instructions on each turn (`instructions` on the Runs API, `system_message` on the session stream). Hermes adds them to the system prompt for that turn only and doesn't store them, so Telegram and the CLI never see them. Settings > Replies and files > Rich replies turns this off. Turns whose reply will be read aloud, in Live mode or after a spoken question in the overlay, go without them, since a card can't be heard.

The agent answers in markdown as usual and adds a fenced `openui-lang` block when structure helps:

````
```openui-lang
root = Card([header, table, followUps])
header = CardHeader("Energy contracts", "Fixed, 1 year")
table = Table([Col("Supplier", ["A", "B"]), Col("Per month", ["€142", "€148"], "number")])
followUps = FollowUpBlock([FollowUpItem("Which one is greenest?")])
```
````

`openui/` holds a Kotlin port of the reference parser in `@openuidev/lang-core` (spec v0.5): statements, references in any order, expressions, `$variables`, `@Each` and the data built-ins. Like the reference, it closes unfinished strings and brackets, so a block renders while it streams in. `ui/openui/` draws the OpenUI chat library's components with their positional arguments in the same order: cards, headers, text, callouts, code, images, tables, bar, line, area, pie and stacked charts, steps, tabs, accordions, lists, tags, buttons, follow-ups and forms with validation. Charts take their colors from a fixed palette checked for colorblind separation on dudan's skies; tap one to read its values, or switch it to a table. A block that doesn't parse, or whose components have nothing to draw, shows as code. Components dudan doesn't draw show their text. Once a reply is complete, parts that draw nothing, such as an empty slide, fold or hidden callout, are left out instead of leaving a gap.

Images in blocks share one memory budget: 64 MB of decoded pictures on screen at once, each decoded at no more than 1280 pixels on its longest side (640 in a gallery). An image that doesn't fit the budget, or doesn't load, shows its alt text or link, and tapping it opens the source. Galleries of up to four images sit in a grid; larger ones scroll sideways and let go of the thumbnails that scroll out of view.

When a button or form submit can't send because a reply is still coming in, dudan asks you to wait and skips the steps after it, such as resetting the form.

Copy, share, the reply notification and read-aloud turn each block into the markdown it stands for: a table stays a table, and steps become a numbered list.

## Scheduled tasks

The clock in the drawer's bottom row opens Scheduled tasks. Each task shows its name, when it runs in a short sentence ("Weekdays at 07:30", "Elke 30 minuten, 07:00–22:59"), its next run on the phone's clock and the first lines of its instruction, with Run now and Pause or Resume. A schedule dudan can't put into words shows as the server wrote it, and a task that runs on another time zone's clock names that zone. A task whose results come to dudan also has Results, once its chat exists, which opens that chat. When Clark held back a task's last results because it runs in shadow mode, the task says so on one line.

New task, at the bottom of the screen, opens the editor; so does tapping a task or its Edit button. The editor has a name, the instruction for the agent, and When:

- Once: a date and a time, or In 1 hour, Tonight 20:00 or Tomorrow 09:00.
- Repeat: every 5, 10, 15, 20 or 30 minutes, or every 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 or 12 hours. It can be limited to the hours between two whole hours of the day (07:00 to 22:59, for example) and to some days of the week.
- Daily, Weekly and Monthly: a time, plus the days of the week or the day of the month. Months without that day are skipped, as cron does.
- Custom: a five-field cron (minute, hour, day of the month, month, day of the week, with Sunday as 0), checked as you type.

Under the picker the editor says what the schedule means and lists its next three runs on the phone's clock, or says why it can't be saved yet, such as a time that has passed or one the clocks skip when summer time starts. Delete asks first. Back asks before it throws away changes, and waits while a save or delete is under way.

dudan sends a schedule as a cron, or for Once as a local date-time with the phone's UTC offset, such as `2026-10-27T09:00:00+01:00`, together with the phone's time zone. An edit sends only the fields that changed. A schedule you don't change isn't sent, so one the picker can't show, like Hermes' `every 90m`, stays as it is when you only change the instruction. Picking what is already picked, or going to another choice and back, is no change. When the server refuses something, such as a cron it doesn't accept, the editor shows its reason above Save.

To edit a task, dudan first loads its whole instruction, since Clark's list cuts it at 600 characters. Until that has loaded, the instruction can't be changed and Save stays off; when loading fails, the editor says why and offers Retry. Only a server without the single-task endpoint falls back to the list's instruction.

A schedule the editor doesn't change keeps running the way the server runs it:

- A Clark task on another time zone's clock, such as Europe/Amsterdam on a phone set to Europe/London, keeps that clock. The editor names the zone after the sentence ("Every day at 09:00 (Europe/Amsterdam)") and shows the runs on the phone's clock (08:00), as the list does. A changed schedule goes out with the phone's time zone, and the editor says so under the preview.
- Hermes counts `every N minutes` on from the task's last run rather than keeping to the clock. For such a task the editor counts its next runs on from the server's next run. A schedule picked instead goes out as a cron that keeps to the clock, and the editor says so.

What each server offers:

- Stock Hermes has all the job endpoints without listing them in `/v1/capabilities`, so dudan offers New task, Edit and Delete. Hermes doesn't take a time zone and runs crons on its own configured clock.
- Clark lists `job_create`, `job_update`, `job_delete` and `job` next to `jobs`. It also sends `delivery_targets`, and the editor then asks where results go: This app, Clark's other destinations, or nowhere, which keeps them in the job log. A new task delivers to its own chat in dudan when Clark offers This app (the target `@dudan`), and otherwise starts on the first destination. Clark names a task by its ID, so an existing task keeps its name. A task Clark won't let the app change, such as one written by hand on the server, shows Clark's reason instead of Edit.
- A Clark from before job management lists `jobs` without `job_create`; dudan then only runs, pauses and resumes.

When dudan can't load the capabilities, it shows the controls and lets the server's answers speak for themselves.

### Results in dudan

A task that delivers to This app gets a chat of its own, named after the task, which Clark makes when the first result comes in and again after you delete it. The drawer marks such a chat with a clock, and a new result moves it to the top. Each result shows as an answer under a label such as "Scheduled task · 07:30", with the date when it came in on another day, and its files show and save like an answer's. A task scheduled from a chat, with Clark's `schedule` tool and `here`, sends its results back to that chat instead; there the label also names the task. A result that comes in while you're in the middle of a turn in that chat stays a separate answer and doesn't take the turn's steps or files. A result that comes in after an answer that failed or was stopped leaves that answer its Retry, and one that comes in after a background result leaves it Review result and finish. You can reply in a task's chat like in any other, and the agent reads the results above your message.

Clark has no way to reach the phone, so dudan checks for new results itself. Every 15 minutes while the phone has a network, Android runs a check that lists the chats and reads the latest rows of each task chat with something new since it last looked. Each new result gets a notification on the Scheduled tasks channel with the task's name and the start of the result; tapping it opens the chat. Opening a task chat counts its results as seen and clears their notifications. While dudan is in front, the chat on screen gets no notification; the drawer and that chat are brought up to date instead. A chat behind another screen, such as Scheduled tasks or Settings, still gets one, and counts as seen once you go back to it. The first check after you install dudan or update it from a version without the check, and the first after you switch servers, count everything until then as seen, so older results aren't reported. The check goes by the server's clock, so a phone clock that runs fast or slow doesn't hide a result. Stock Hermes has no task chats, and there the check finds nothing.

## Known limits

- [Agent screen](agent-screen.md) requires desktop protocol version 1 from a compatible Clark server. Hermes servers without it show no desktop controls. Watching uses PNG polling; video streaming, recording and teaching are not implemented. After losing control or reconnecting, the agent stays paused until Return control. If its model turn already ended, send a continuation message after handing back.
- [Files returned by Clark](outbound-attachments.md) need a Clark server that sends attachment metadata; with Hermes, answers carry no files. The side-key overlay doesn't show an answer's files; open the chat in the app to see and save them. Clark keeps the bytes as long as its retention policy says, so an older file can show as no longer available.

- Turns with photos use the session chat stream, because that is where Hermes validates image parts. Those turns stop if the connection drops mid-run. Turns with other files are text and use the Runs API.
- Files other than photos need the upload service on the Hermes host. The model never sees them directly: the agent opens them with its tools, so what it can do with a video or a spreadsheet depends on the tools and skills Hermes has.
- Scheduled results are checked for every 15 minutes at best. Android can wait longer while the phone dozes or saves battery, and Samsung's battery optimizations can stop the check; set dudan's battery usage to Unrestricted if notifications come late. Only a task's own chat is checked: a result that goes back to the chat a task was scheduled from shows up when you open that chat, without a notification. The check reads a chat's latest 40 rows, so when more results than that came in since it last looked, the older ones get no notification.
- Background results show up live, with a notification, only while the app process runs. Hermes has no push channel for them, so when Android has stopped dudan or keeps a sleeping phone offline, the result appears the next time you open the chat or return to the app, without a notification. Nothing continues the task until you tap Review result and finish; continuing without the phone would have to happen on the Hermes server.
- When two phones with dudan both tap Review result and finish, they share one run, because the run carries an idempotency key named after the result. Telegram or another client continuing the chat in the same moment can still start a turn of its own; only Hermes could rule that out.
- A chat shows its latest 500 rows, Hermes' default page. Background results land at the end, so they always show. After a restart, dudan reads older pages to find background work that is still out, but only work started in the last six hours; results of older work appear when you open the chat, not live.
- Uploads run while dudan is open. When Android stops the app during a large upload, pick the file again; the service deletes the abandoned part after 24 hours.
- Hermes' API server doesn't expand skills in a message the way Telegram's `/skill-name` does, so the agent loads each tagged skill itself, with one `skill_view` call per skill. Hermes puts a turn's instructions in the system prompt, ahead of the chat history, so a turn with tags can't reuse the provider's prompt cache for that history.
- Hermes doesn't say which time zone it runs a cron in. The editor works out a Hermes task's next runs on the phone's clock, so for a Hermes set to another time zone they differ from the real runs by the zones' difference.
- Stock Hermes answers the skill list with HTTP 500 until upstream merges a one-line fix. Without the list, the Skills screen explains the error and `$` tags stay plain text. See [Skills](setup.md#skills).
- OpenUI blocks can't fetch data: `Query` renders its defaults, and `Mutation` and `@Run` do nothing. dudan draws the chat library's common components; card blocks such as `SnippetCardBlock` show their text without their layout, and icons cover a few dozen lucide names.
- The wake word only listens while the phone is unlocked with the screen on, and pauses during calls and while voice input records. On a locked phone it stays off on purpose: the agent can act on the phone and on its server, so anyone near the phone could use it. The microphone stays open while it listens, so Android shows its microphone dot and dudan keeps a notification up. The spotter knows English sounds only: a phrase works best as two or three words spelled the way they sound in English, and can have at most five. It needs dudan to be the default assistant to open the overlay and to come back after a restart.
- Only Orukeet gets the audio right after the wake phrase. The Android recognizer, when it's chosen under Speech input or while Orukeet isn't installed yet, records in its own process and starts a moment after the overlay opens, so a question asked straight after the phrase is cut off or lost. After a wake word it plays a short tone when it starts listening; wait for it before you ask. When it hears nothing at all, it listens once more, with the tone again. Settings > Assistant > Wake word says so while Orukeet isn't ready.
- "Hey Google" and "Hi Bixby" listen without the microphone dot because they come with the phone. Android keeps the low-power wake-word hardware and the hotword audio source for preinstalled apps (`MANAGE_HOTWORD_DETECTION` is `internal|preinstalled`, `CAPTURE_AUDIO_HOTWORD` is `signature|privileged|role`, and the assistant role grants neither), so an installed app's wake word has to keep the microphone open. Clark in the app list hands the waiting to Bixby instead. That hasn't been tested on a Samsung phone yet: whether Bixby finds the Clark entry by its name, and whether One UI shows a dot while Bixby waits.
- When voice input can't start (the microphone is busy, Orukeet's voice detector fails to load), the overlay falls back to the Android recognizer and tries again twice before it leaves you with the keyboard. During a call Orukeet doesn't fall back, since any recording would only hear the call. Only silence ends listening without another try, apart from the one after a wake word on the Android recognizer (above).
- The side-key overlay needs the microphone permission granted once in the app. Without it, the overlay opens the app to ask.
- Kokoro synthesizes a whole sentence before it plays it, so a long first sentence delays the start of speech. On the x86 emulator a sentence of about 3 seconds starts playing 1.7 to 2.1 seconds after the request once the model is loaded; a phone's arm64 cores are faster, but this hasn't been measured on the Fold 7 yet.
- Orukeet transcribes after you stop talking, with previews of the text so far. Two seconds of silence ends a question; Settings > Speech input > Stop listening after sets anything from 1 to 5 seconds. The Android recognizer gets the same value as a request, but it may keep its own timing. Voice typing in other apps still goes to the phone's recognizer.
- Orukeet has no language setting of its own. With the speech language set to Dutch or English, dudan decodes up to 4 seconds of speech right after a recorded sentence in that language (about 1.7 seconds, then 0.4 seconds of silence) and drops that sentence's words from the transcript. Without it, "Hoe laat is het?" could come out as "Who laat is it?". An English sentence still comes through when Dutch is set. On a PC the extra audio adds about 150 ms to a 2-second question; on a phone it's more, but this hasn't been measured on the Fold 7 yet. Longer speech is decoded on its own, because behind the recorded sentence Orukeet sometimes skipped whole sentences of it; when that comes out in the other language, it's decoded once more behind the recorded sentence, and that wins if it's in the chosen language. Other languages have no recorded sentence, so Orukeet still works those out itself. The setting can be written as `nl-NL`, `nl`, `Dutch` or `Nederlands`; a tag with a script or variant, such as `sr-Latn` or `ca-ES-valencia`, is passed on whole. The Android recognizer gets it with its own language detection turned off, and spoken turns ask the agent to answer in that language.
- Supertonic synthesizes a sentence before it plays it. On the x86 emulator the first word of a Dutch reply comes 1.4 seconds after the request once the model is loaded; this hasn't been measured on the Fold 7 yet. When a very short first sentence ("Goede vraag.") is followed by a long one, a pause can fall between them while the long one is synthesized.
- Kokoro and Supertonic loaded together take about 1.1 GB of memory, and Orukeet adds its own while listening. Each is released after a few idle minutes.
- If Kokoro's or Supertonic's native library ever crashes the app while loading, the next start notices, hands those replies to the Android voice (on Automatic, Supertonic takes English when it's installed) and shows a Retry link in Settings > Voices.
- Supertonic reads digits as they are in every language but Dutch, and times and amounts can come out wrong: in German, "12,50 €" came back from speech recognition as "12 von 50". Plain numbers such as "10 Uhr" are fine.
- The language guess goes by a reply's words, so a reply that mixes languages is read in one voice. Close neighbors (Czech and Slovak, Croatian and Slovenian) can get each other's voice, and Norwegian, which Supertonic doesn't read, can get the Danish one.
- Phone control can't open apps on a locked phone; they show after unlocking, and the tool tells the agent so. It can't read the screen or tap things in other apps either. That would need an accessibility service, which dudan doesn't have.
- The phone must be on Tailscale for Hermes to reach it. Samsung's battery optimizations can stop the service; set dudan's battery usage to Unrestricted if Hermes loses the phone while it sleeps.
- Dialogs, sheets and the side-key overlay frost the screen behind them with Android's cross-window blur. When a phone turns that off (some battery savers do), they switch to solid panels over the sharp background. Menus are popups, which can't blur what's behind them, so they are nearly opaque.
